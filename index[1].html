Legal BackgroundThe Ruling & What It Means

On May 27, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia ruled that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) lacked statutory authority to include 20 of its 42 Medicare Advantage Star Rating measures in plan performance calculations. The ruling ordered CMS to recalculate Clover Insurance Company's Star Rating without those measures.

"The financial architecture of Medicare Advantage just shifted under every plan that bid on QBP assumptions."

CMS filed for reconsideration the following day. The court denied it. The agency now faces a choice: appeal to the Eleventh Circuit, seek a legislative fix from Congress, or comply and accept that its Star Ratings program is structurally vulnerable to challenge.

For the broader MA market, the ruling creates a litigation roadmap. Any plan that performs poorly on measures touching CMS's contested statutory authority now has a viable legal theory for challenge. The 2027 MA bid deadline was June 1, four days after the ruling, meaning plans submitted bids with pre-ruling QBP assumptions that may no longer hold.

For I-SNP plans specifically, the seven invalidated measures that disproportionately affect frail elderly institutionalized populations represent a clinical integrity correction as much as a legal one. These populations were being measured against benchmarks designed for healthier community-dwelling members.

Population-Specific AnalysisI-SNP Clinical Impact

Institutional Special Needs Plans serve frail elderly members in long-term care settings. Seven of the 20 invalidated measures disproportionately burdened I-SNP plans with benchmarks calibrated to healthier populations.

Medication Adherence Measures

Diabetes, hypertension, and cholesterol adherence measures carry weight 3 and assume community-based pharmacy access and cognitive capacity inconsistent with I-SNP populations.

Blood Pressure & Glucose Control

Control thresholds for hypertension and diabetes in frail elderly populations with multiple comorbidities are clinically distinct from community benchmarks. One-size metrics disadvantage appropriate clinical management.

Statin Use in Older Adults

Deprescribing statins in frail elderly patients with limited life expectancy reflects evidence-based practice, not underperformance. This measure penalized clinically appropriate care decisions.

Legal AnalysisStatutory Authority Challenge
Core Holding: CMS exceeded its statutory authority under 42 U.S.C. § 1395w-23 in selecting and weighting the 20 invalidated measures. The court found the agency's rulemaking process failed to satisfy the arbitrary and capricious standard under the APA for these specific measures.
Market Precedent: The ruling creates a reproducible legal theory for any MA plan performing poorly on measures touching CMS's contested authority. Plans with Star Ratings near 4.0 thresholds now have financial incentive to evaluate litigation options before the 2027 rating cycle.
FCA Exposure: Plans that received QBP bonuses based on inflated Star Ratings derived from now-invalidated measures may face retroactive scrutiny. The intersection of Stars methodology and bonus payment certifications warrants monitoring as the case develops.
